How Does Traffic Prediction Work?

Both models predict hourly traffic volume using four inputs from historical traffic and weather records:

Time of day

The hour, from 0 to 23. Helps identify morning and evening traffic patterns.

Humidity

Relative humidity in the weather data, which may relate to driving conditions.

Temperature

Air temperature, measured in Kelvin in the source dataset.

Cloud coverage

Percentage of the sky covered by clouds, from 0% to 100%.

Quantum model: Encodes these four inputs into a four-qubit circuit using PennyLane and the Amazon Braket local simulator. Circuit measurements are combined to predict traffic.

Random Forest: Uses the same inputs with conventional decision trees to generate a traffic prediction.

Prediction closeness compares the total prediction error with the total actual traffic volume. Higher is better; 100% means perfect predictions. Values can be negative when prediction errors are exceptionally large.

MAE is the average prediction error measured in vehicles. Lower is better. Both metrics are calculated from the running historical replay, which can include training records, rather than an independent held-out test.
